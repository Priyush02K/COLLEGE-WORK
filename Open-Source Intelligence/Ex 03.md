# Experiment 03

## Aim

To create and execute OSINT search queries (dorks) to find publicly available information and **cross-reference multiple sources** to verify the accuracy, reliability, and credibility of a news article.

---

## 1. Objectives

After completing this experiment, students will be able to:

- Understand OSINT dorks and advanced search operators.
- Create targeted search queries for information verification.
- Extract important claims from a news article.
- Search for supporting and contradicting evidence.
- Cross-reference multiple independent sources.
- Identify primary and secondary sources.
- Evaluate the reliability and credibility of information.
- Classify claims as verified, partially verified, unverified, or contradicted.

---

## 2. What are OSINT Dorks?

An **OSINT dork** is a specially constructed search query that uses advanced search operators to locate specific information available on the public Internet.

Instead of a broad search:

```text
cyber attack company
```

an investigator can use:

```text
site:example.com "cyber attack"
```

> **Important:** OSINT dorks are search techniques for locating publicly accessible information. They should not be used to bypass authentication, access restricted systems, or obtain unauthorized information.

---

## 3. Common OSINT Search Operators

| Operator | Purpose | Example |
|---|---|---|
| `" "` | Search for an exact phrase | `"cyber attack"` |
| `site:` | Search within a specific website/domain | `site:example.com security` |
| `intitle:` | Search for terms in a page title | `intitle:"cyber attack"` |
| `inurl:` | Search for terms in a URL | `inurl:report cybersecurity` |
| `filetype:` | Search for a specific file type | `filetype:pdf cybersecurity` |
| `-` | Exclude a term | `cybersecurity -advertisement` |
| `OR` | Search for either term | `cyber attack OR data breach` |

## 6. Step 2 — Extract Claims from the Article

Read the article carefully and identify its major factual claims.

### Example

Suppose the article states:

> "Company ABC suffered a major cyberattack affecting 100,000 customers."

Possible claims:

| Claim | Information to Verify |
|---|---|
| Company ABC suffered a cyberattack | Did the attack actually occur? |
| Attack was major | What evidence supports this description? |
| 100,000 customers were affected | Is the number supported? |
| Customer information was exposed | What information was affected? |
| Company confirmed the incident | Did the company officially confirm it? |
| Incident occurred on a particular date | Is the date correct? |

---

## 7. Step 3 — Create Basic Search Queries

Start with targeted searches:

```text
"Company ABC" cyber attack
```

```text
"Company ABC" data breach
```

```text

```

```text

```

Quotation marks can help search for an exact phrase.

## 10. Step 6 — Cross-Reference Multiple News Sources

Search the same claim independently:

```text
"Company ABC" cyber attack
```

Compare:

- News organization A
- News organization B
- News organization C
- Official company source
- Government/regulatory source, if applicable

> Several websites repeating the same original report do not necessarily represent independent confirmation.

## 15. Step 11 — Evaluate Source Reliability

For every source, ask:

### A. Who published it?

- Official organization
- Government agency
- Regulatory body
- Established news organization
- Industry publication
- Unknown website
- Anonymous social media account

### B. Is evidence provided?

Check for:

- Official statement
- Original document
- Named source
- Technical evidence
- Photographs/videos
- Links to primary sources

### C. Is the information current?

Check:

- Publication date
- Updated date
- Incident date
- Date of referenced evidence

### D. Is the claim independently confirmed?

Determine whether other sources independently support the claim.

---

## 16. Source Reliability Classification

| Reliability Level | Examples |
|---|---|
| **High** | Official government sources, regulatory filings, official organizational statements, original reports |
| **Medium** | Established industry publications, reputable technology publications, established news organizations |
| **Low / Unverified** | Unknown blogs, anonymous posts, unsourced websites, reposted content without attribution |

> Reliability must be evaluated for the specific claim. Even reputable sources can make errors, so important claims should be checked against primary evidence where possible.

---

## 17. Step 12 — Identify Contradictions

Example:

```text
Article A:
100,000 customers affected

Article B:
50,000 customers affected

Official statement:
Number not confirmed
```

Do not simply select one number.

Instead:

```text
Conflicting Information
          |
          v
Find Primary Evidence
          |
          v
Check Original Statement
          |
          v
Determine What Can Be Confirmed
```

If the number cannot be confirmed, classify it as **Unverified**.

---

## 18. Step 13 — Classify Each Claim

| Classification | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Verified** | Supported by reliable evidence and independent/primary sources |
| **Partially Verified** | Some parts are supported, but important details remain uncertain |
| **Unverified** | No reliable supporting evidence was found |
| **Contradicted** | Reliable evidence indicates that the claim is incorrect |

## 23. Example Final Assessment

> The main claim in the article was supported by an official organizational statement and multiple independent reputable sources. However, the reported number of affected customers could not be independently confirmed. Therefore, the main incident claim is supported, while the numerical claim should be treated with caution.

---

## 24. Precautions and Ethical Guidelines

1. Use only publicly accessible information.
2. Do not bypass authentication or access controls.
3. Do not attempt to access restricted systems or files.
4. Do not use search operators to obtain unauthorized access.
5. Do not collect unnecessary personal information.
6. Do not treat search results as automatically accurate.
7. Cross-reference important claims with reliable sources.
8. Prefer primary sources whenever available.
9. Record publication dates and source details.
10. Respect privacy, copyright, institutional policies, and applicable laws.
